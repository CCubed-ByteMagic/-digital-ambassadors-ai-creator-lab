Why Your AI Prompt
Didn't Work

A short creative experiment

We wanted AI video to create a capital letter C from grain on the ocean floor. The first two attempts missed the idea. The third worked. Here is what changed—and why.

01

First attempt

The idea is there, but the shape is not behaving as intended.

02

Second attempt

Adding “horizontal” still does not clearly define the view or shape.

03

Third attempt: success

Clearer camera direction and a flatter shape communicate the intention more precisely.

IT WORKED
THE EXPERIMENT

What was going wrong?

The original prompt asked for grain to gather into a “mound shaped like the capital letter C.” Then we added the word “horizontal.” But the model still made the C stand upright.

The problem was not simply a missing adjective. Words such as horizontal and mound can leave room for different interpretations. The prompt had not clearly explained what the camera should see or how the grain should lie on the seabed.

When an AI result is wrong, first look for the ambiguity. Don't just add more words.
↳   THE IMPROVED DESCRIPTION

“The camera splits underwater, then tilts to look straight down at the white sandy seabed. The grain settles and spreads into a low, flat layer lying on the sand, forming the capital letter ‘C’ like a letter drawn on the ground, readable from directly above.”

✓   Clearer camera angle + clearer physical shape
WHAT WE CAN LEARN
01

Name the camera view

“Look straight down” is more specific than hoping the model guesses the angle.

02

Describe the physical result

“Low, flat layer” removes the unwanted impression of a tall mound.

03

Change the cause, not everything

Identify the confusing part and make a targeted revision. Then test again.

✳
YOUR TURN

Try this the next time AI gets it wrong.

Ask yourself three questions: What do I want to see? From which angle? What should the objects physically do? Rewrite only the unclear part, then test again.
